## 8. Long-term portfolio implications

### Indian equities: Foreign capital rotation

**Potential structural opportunity**

The November inflow trend is important because India is receiving capital while international investors reduce exposure to expensive technology-linked markets.

However, one must distinguish between:

- Structural foreign allocation to Indian domestic growth.
- Tactical rotation away from AI and global technology stocks.

The first can support long-term earnings multiples. The second can reverse quickly if global sentiment improves or India valuations become relatively expensive.

### Banking and financial stocks

The combination of strong bank-stock performance and lower bond yields is supportive.

However, the inflation breach and expected RBI rate increases create an important medium-term challenge.

Focus on:

- Deposit growth versus credit growth.
- CASA trends.
- Net interest margins.
- Credit costs.
- Unsecured lending exposure.
- Governance and risk management.

A stronger economy does not automatically translate into stronger banking-sector returns if funding costs and asset quality deteriorate.

### Energy and oil-related investments

Lower crude prices are constructive for India's macroeconomic fundamentals.

Potential benefits include:

- Reduced import bill.
- Better current-account dynamics.
- Lower inflation pressure.
- Improved margins for selected fuel-consuming companies.

However, upstream oil producers may face lower realisations.

The Iran conflict creates an asymmetric risk: crude could fall gradually with weaker demand but rise sharply following an interruption to shipping or supply.

### Gold and portfolio hedging

Gold should continue to be considered as a diversification asset rather than simply a short-term trading instrument.

The latest price correction demonstrates that even safe-haven assets can experience significant drawdowns when real yields and monetary-policy expectations change.

Portfolio exposure should reflect its role as a hedge, not an assumption of continuously rising prices.

### Infrastructure and industrials

The 10.9% core infrastructure output growth is a constructive structural indicator.

The combination of strong cement, steel, coal, electricity and iron ore production supports the broader industrial and infrastructure investment thesis.

Nevertheless, stock selection should focus on order execution, return on capital, leverage and cash flows rather than headline sector growth alone.

### AI and technology: Temporary correction or structural change?

This is arguably the most important global medium-term development.

The immediate market correction reflects doubts about the financial returns on massive AI investments.

There are two competing possibilities:

1. **Healthy valuation correction:** AI demand remains strong, but investors demand better cash-flow visibility and more disciplined capital expenditure.
2. **Structural investment slowdown:** Debt-funded data-centre expansion slows, affecting semiconductor orders, technology suppliers, power infrastructure and global corporate investment.

Indian IT services companies may also face valuation pressure, although their business models and exposure differ from semiconductor and data-centre infrastructure companies.

The distinction will matter significantly over the next 3–12 months.
